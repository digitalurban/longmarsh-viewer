Longmarsh Green Energy: solar farm, battery storage, overhead lines and a 1 GW data centre campus proposed across 1,980 ha between Walpole and Barton Bendish.

Getting around the map

  • Move: drag the map. Zoom: scroll, pinch, or use the + and − buttons (bottom right).
  • Tilt and turn (3D): right-click and drag, or hold Ctrl and drag. On a phone or tablet, drag up or down with two fingers to tilt, and twist to turn. The compass button (bottom right) turns the map back to north.
  • OpenStreetMap / Aerial (top right) switches between a street map and aerial photography.
  • 2D / 3D (top right) flattens the map or raises each zone to its maximum published height.
  • On a phone, tap the grey bar at the top of this panel to shrink or expand it.

Finding your way round the scheme

  • Map tab: the buttons Whole scheme, Western Site & data centre, Eastern Site and Walpole take you to each area on the flat (2D) map. Press 3D (top right) to raise the heights.
  • Layers: tick or untick any item in the Map tab to show or hide it. Published layers come from the developer's plans. Awaiting information layers are presumptions where details haven't been published.
  • Official data (Map tab) adds national maps of flood risk, farmland grade, heritage and protected wildlife sites from the Environment Agency, Defra, Historic England and Natural England. Tap any feature for what it is, how far it is from the site and why it matters. Tap a coloured area to see what it means.
  • Other solar projects (Map tab) shows The Droves and High Grove, two separate national solar projects to the east, for context. Tick or untick them in the legend.
  • Click any shape to see what it is, its maximum height, its source document and what is still unknown. The Near me tab shows that spot's distances to each part of the scheme.

Checking your own area

  • Near me tab: enter a postcode, or press Use my location (your browser will ask permission first). You'll see straight-line distances to the solar areas, the data centre zone, the substations, the overhead line and the site boundary.
  • Write a response letter (Near me tab, after a postcode) drafts a letter built from your location, the concerns you choose and your own words, ready to print, sign and post. Check and edit it before you send it.
  • From there, Street View facing it opens Google Street View at that spot, pointing towards the nearest part of the scheme. Stand here in 3D drops you to eye level on this map.
  • Villages tab: 45 nearby villages, sorted by distance to the site boundary. Tap one to zoom to it on the flat map, framed with the nearest part of the scheme, and see its distances. Use Stand here in 3D for an eye-level view.

Reading and responding

  • Facts: key figures, maximum heights, design commitments and the timeline.
  • Awaiting: what the developer hasn't published yet, and the presumptions this map makes.
  • Objections: issues people may raise, with what the documents say and the developer's position.
  • Respond: deadlines, postal and email addresses for the scheme consultation and the government's data centre policy consultation.
  • Documents: links to the official documents online.
  • Local info: parish council pages and local campaigns about Longmarsh.
  • Download (Map tab): the map data as GeoJSON, for use in GIS software.

Presumptions this map makes

  • Each built zone is raised to its published maximum height across its whole area. These are the same heights the applicant used for its visibility study (ZTV).
  • The overhead line is drawn down the middle of its 100 m corridor.
  • Towers are spaced every ~350 m (West) and ~300 m (East). This is for illustration only. No tower positions or spacings have been published.
  • The 300 m line is measured from the edge of each zone. The commitment applies to the buildings themselves, which haven't been placed yet.

Loss of prime farmland (best and most versatile land)In the documents

The issueMuch of the site is predicted to be "best and most versatile" (BMV) farmland, which policy defines as Grades 1, 2 and 3a. The draft data centre policy says poorer-quality land should be used where possible.

What the documents sayWalpole substation land is all predicted Grade 1. Most of the Western Site, where the data centre goes, is Grade 2 and 3a, with some Grade 1. The Eastern Cable Route is mostly Grade 2 and 3a, with Grade 1 east of the River Great Ouse. The Eastern Site is Grade 2 and 3b in the west and mostly 3a in the east. These are predictive grades from a national map; the developer plans field soil surveys. The draft data centre policy says that if significant development of farmland is necessary, poorer land (3b, 4, 5) should be used where possible, and that the Secretary of State should ensure higher-quality land has been avoided where possible. The borough's Local Plan (Policy LP24, as quoted in the Scoping Report) says the council will seek to protect productive and BMV land, and that applications for other uses that would adversely affect it are likely to be refused unless the benefits outweigh its loss.

Official map, measuredOn Defra's Predictive ALC Map for England (the map the Scoping Report uses), about 87% of the land inside the site boundary is predicted to be BMV: roughly 9% Grade 1, 38% Grade 2 and 40% Grade 3a, with 11% Grade 3b. About 85% of the solar areas and all of the data centre zone are predicted BMV. These are this viewer's measurements of a predictive map, not field survey results. Turn on Agricultural land grade in the Map tab to see it.

Developer\'s positionAbout 85% of land within 6.7 km of Walpole substation, and about 95% within 20 km, is BMV, so there is little alternative near the grid connection. After 60 years the solar land would be restored so it could return to farming.

Points to raiseThe data centre, Western substation and batteries, Walpole substation and Western overhead line are treated as permanent development, so no return to farming is proposed for that land. Ask for the field-survey results, the area of each grade under permanent and under temporary development, and evidence that poorer land was considered. National food security is also in scope: the developer has said it will assess it.

Scoping Report §14.4.5–14.4.6, §4.5.4, §5.6.4 (KL&WN Local Plan Policy LP24); draft Data Centres NPS paras 362 and 379

Site chosen because the land was available (land ownership)Subject to clarification

The issueThe published documents do not say who owns the land. It has been suggested locally that much of it may be in a single ownership. This has not been confirmed and should not be relied on.

What the documents saySite selection was influenced by grid capacity at Walpole, "availability of suitable land", distance from population centres and access to A-roads. The developer says it is holding "landowner discussions" and refers to "landowners" in the plural.

Developer's positionAlternative locations, routes and designs, and the reasons for its choices, will be set out in the Environmental Statement.

Points to raiseWho owns land is not normally a planning reason in itself. The useful argument is about alternatives: if the site was picked because the land was easy to get rather than because it is the best place, the developer should show what other sites were assessed. If the application seeks compulsory acquisition powers, it must include Land Plans and a Book of Reference listing the owners and other interests in the land. In the meantime, you can ask the developer to confirm the position.

Scoping Report §2.4.3, §16.6.5; local suggestion (unverified)

Data centre added to an energy projectIn the documents

The issueA data centre is not automatically a nationally significant project. It can enter this process only through a direction from the Secretary of State under Section 35 of the Planning Act 2008, so that it can be decided in the same application as the solar farm.

What the documents sayThe developer has submitted a request for a Section 35 direction for the data centre campus. The draft policy says data centres of at least 100 MW of IT load are likely to be considered suitable for direction. That policy is still a draft: its consultation closes 8 January 2027 and Parliament's scrutiny runs to 17 February 2027.

Developer's positionCo-locating the campus means solar power reaches it at low cost, which reduces demand on the grid.

Points to raiseWhether a 1 GW hyperscale data centre on farmland should be decided together with a solar scheme, and under a policy that has not yet been finalised. Make this point in both consultations (see the Respond tab).

Scoping Report §1.4.5; draft Data Centres NPS paras 24–25 and 39; Leaflet p12

Scale and permanence of the data centreIn the documents

The issueThis is a hyperscale campus of about 1 GW: up to 10 buildings up to 25 m tall (including rooftop plant) on up to 70 ha, built in phases over about 10 years from 2029.

What the documents sayThe data centre is treated as permanent, together with the Western substation, batteries and the Western overhead line. If the data centre doesn't go ahead, the area would be used for more solar.

Developer's positionIt sits at least 300 m from homes and will be screened by landscaping.

Points to raiseIts height and bulk in a flat, open Fen landscape; a construction period of about 10 years; continuous operation; and whether a 300 m separation is enough for buildings of this size.

Scoping Report §4.4.35–4.4.45, §4.5.4, §4.6.3; Leaflet p17, p20

Flood risk on the Western SiteIn the documents

The issueMost of the Western Site, where the data centre is proposed, is in the Environment Agency's highest tidal flood zone.

What the documents sayMost of the Western Site is in Flood Zone 3 (tidal), with a small area of Zone 2 at its southern end and one area of Zone 1 near its centre. The Western Cable Route is entirely in Zones 2 and 3, and the Walpole land almost entirely. The draft data centre policy says the Secretary of State must be satisfied that the Sequential and Exception Tests have been applied where required.

Official map, measuredOn the Environment Agency's Flood Map for Planning (updated 22 October 2026), about 42% of the land inside the whole site boundary is in Flood Zone 3, including almost all of the data centre zone and about 37% of the solar areas. Flood zones ignore defences. These are this viewer's measurements; turn on Flood Zones in the Map tab to see the map.

Developer\'s positionFlood risk will be assessed with the Environment Agency, the Lead Local Flood Authority and the Internal Drainage Boards.

Surface waterFlood zones cover rivers and the sea only. Flooding from heavy rain is mapped separately in the Environment Agency's Risk of Flooding from Surface Water, which the Scoping Report lists as a data source. It shows rainfall flow paths and ponding across parts of the Eastern Site and around Fincham. Turn on Surface water flood risk in the Map tab and zoom in to see it.

Points to raiseExactly where the data centre buildings sit in relation to the flood zones (including the Zone 1 area), how the Sequential Test has been applied, and how the buildings would stay safe over their lifetime.

Scoping Report §13.4.52; draft Data Centres NPS para 312

Landscape and new overhead linesIn the documents

The issueAbout 14 km of new overhead line: about 7 km on towers up to 50 m, and about 7 km on towers up to 35 m. Add about 1,400 ha of solar areas (measured from the layout plan) with panels up to 4.5 m high, fencing and CCTV poles.

What the documents sayThe visibility study (ZTV) used towers of 50 m and 35 m and a 25 m data centre. The Western line would run between existing overhead lines. The Eastern line runs south of the existing Walpole–Norwich line.

Developer's positionGrouping the new line with existing lines limits new effects. Sensitive heritage areas would be crossed underground.

Points to raiseAsk for photomontages from your own viewpoints, and for the underground option to be considered for longer stretches.

Scoping Report §4.4.47–4.4.52, §10.3

Heritage settingsIn the documents

The issueThe scheme is close to designated heritage assets.

What the documents sayStradsett Hall Registered Park and Garden (Grade II) is about 0.1 km west of the site. The underground cable section is intended to avoid it and the Grade I Church of St Botolph. The developer expects 11 scheduled monuments, 9 Grade I and 11 Grade II* listed buildings, Fincham Conservation Area and Stradsett Hall park to go forward for detailed assessment. Scheduled monuments near the site include the bowl barrow known as The Mound, about 200 m south of it.

Official map, measuredOn Historic England's records there are about 76 listed buildings within 1 km of the site boundary, including 6 Grade I and 8 Grade II*, and about 243 within 3 km. The nearest are Grade II buildings at Playters Hall and Grays Hall, about 15–25 m away. Fincham Hall (Grade II*) is about 100 m from the site, and the Grade I Church of St Martin, Fincham, about 290 m. The scheduled moated site and garden remains at Aylmer Hall touch the site boundary on the Western Site, and the Barton Bendish medieval settlement remains are about 140 m away. Fincham Conservation Area touches the boundary, and Shouldham Thorpe's is about 150 m away. These are this viewer's measurements; turn on Heritage in the Map tab and tap any marker.

Why it mattersListed buildings, scheduled monuments, registered parks and conservation areas are protected by law and national policy, and harm to their setting (how they are experienced in their surroundings) is a material planning consideration even when nothing is built on them. Great weight must be given to their conservation, and the more important the asset, the greater the weight.

Developer's positionThere are no designated assets within the site. The Aylmer Hall scheduled monument has been kept outside the boundary, and related earthworks under an existing farm track would be left in place. Underground cabling, setbacks and screening reduce effects on their settings.

Points to raiseEffects on the setting of these assets, and on undesignated heritage and archaeology. An archaeology strategy is promised.

Scoping Report §4.4.48, §10.3.10–10.3.13, §11.5.2–11.5.3, §11.6.23; Leaflet p9, p19; Historic England National Heritage List (measured)

Wildlife: Islington Heronry SSSIIn the documents

The issueA Site of Special Scientific Interest lies inside the Western Site boundary.

What the documents sayIslington Heronry SSSI is within the site, with a minimum 100 m buffer to above-ground infrastructure.

Other SSSIs nearbyThe Scoping Report lists Setchey SSSI (about 10 m from the site, geological), Boughton Fen SSSI (about 25 m, across Oxborough Road), River Nar SSSI (about 0.5 km), Wiggenhall St Germans SSSI (about 0.6 km, geological) and Breckland Farmland SSSI (about 2.6 km, stone curlew). Turn on SSSIs in the Map tab to see them.

Developer's positionThere will be no direct loss of the SSSI, and the scheme will seek to deliver a significant biodiversity net gain.

Points to raiseWhether a 100 m buffer is enough to protect the heronry from construction disturbance, operational noise and lighting.

Scoping Report §4.2.1, §9.6.7, §10.3.17; Leaflet p10

Wildlife: protected sites of international importanceIn the documents

The issueThe scheme is within a few kilometres of wildlife sites protected under the Habitats Regulations, the strongest protection in UK law.

What the documents sayThe Breckland Special Protection Area is about 2.1 km from the site at its closest point, designated for breeding stone curlew, nightjar and woodlark. The Norfolk Valley Fens Special Area of Conservation is about 2.6 km away. Winter bird surveys looked for species from Breckland and The Wash SPAs that may feed or roost on the arable fields. A Habitats Regulations Assessment will be coordinated with the environmental assessment.

Official map, measuredOn Natural England's maps, Breckland SPA is about 2.1 km away, Norfolk Valley Fens SAC about 2.6 km, and The Wash (SPA, SAC and Ramsar site) about 7 km. Turn on Wildlife sites in the Map tab to see them.

Why it mattersA project that could significantly affect these sites, including through birds that use farmland outside the boundaries ("functionally linked land"), can only go ahead if a Habitats Regulations Assessment shows it won't harm the site's integrity, or in tightly defined exceptional circumstances.

Points to raiseThe survey results for stone curlew and other SPA birds on and near the fields, whether any of the land is functionally linked to the SPAs, and how lighting, noise and construction would be controlled.

Scoping Report §2.8.1, §9.4.14, Table 9-3, §10.3.15–10.3.16; Natural England designated sites (measured)

Ancient woodland next to the siteIn the documents

The issueBirch Wood, ancient semi-natural woodland of about 3 ha, sits right on the Eastern Site boundary.

What the documents sayThere is no ancient woodland within the site. The closest is Birch Wood, adjacent to the site boundary in the Eastern Site.

Official map, measuredOn Natural England's Ancient Woodland Inventory, Birch Wood's edge meets the boundary and one of the published solar areas. Several other ancient woods are within 3 km, the nearest (Kippers Wood and Bowl Wood) about 0.7 km away.

Why it mattersNational policy treats ancient woodland as irreplaceable: development that would lose or damage it should be refused unless there are wholly exceptional reasons. Natural England and the Forestry Commission's standing advice is to keep a buffer of at least 15 m, and more where effects such as lighting or drainage changes reach further.

Points to raiseWhat buffer will be kept between Birch Wood and the panels, fencing, tracks and lighting, and how its drainage and root zone will be protected during construction.

Scoping Report §9.4.20; Natural England Ancient Woodland Inventory (measured); NPPF; Natural England and Forestry Commission standing advice on ancient woodland

Noise, lighting and air qualityIn the documents

The issueData centres operate continuously and include cooling infrastructure, backup generators and security lighting.

What the documents sayBackup generators will be assessed as diesel, the worst case. The data centre will need extra CCTV, security and external lighting. High-voltage areas, including substations and batteries, will have lighting but will not be permanently lit.

Developer's positionThe remote location and distance from homes limit effects. Generators run only for emergencies and testing.

Points to raiseAsk for night-time noise limits at named homes, generator testing hours, and lighting levels, especially across flat open land where light travels far.

Scoping Report §4.4.38–4.4.39, §4.4.53–4.4.54; Leaflet p17, p20

Traffic and constructionIn the documents

The issueConstruction is expected to start in 2029, with the solar operating by 2032. The data centre would be built in phases over about 10 years, to around 2039. New site accesses would be created.

What the documents sayHGV routes will avoid Tilney St Lawrence, Barton Bendish and Fincham "where possible". Construction routes and access points are still being developed.

Developer's positionAn outline Construction Traffic Management Plan will be included in the application.

Points to raiseAsk for firm commitments on routes and hours, not "where possible". Raise specific junctions, lanes and school routes.

Scoping Report §4.2.1, §4.6; Leaflet p4, p20

Battery fire safetyIn the documents

The issueBattery storage is planned at both the Eastern and Western Sites.

What the documents sayThey will comply with National Fire Chiefs Council guidance on spacing, emergency access and water supply. Capacity is not yet published.

Developer's positionBatteries let the scheme store solar power and support the grid.

Points to raiseAsk for capacity in MWh, distances to homes and watercourses, fire-water containment, and Norfolk Fire & Rescue's views.

Scoping Report §4.4.28–4.4.33

How much power the data centre actually takes from solarIn the documents

The issueSolar panels generate nothing at night and less in winter, while a data centre operates continuously.

What the documents sayThe scheme would provide up to 1.3–1.4 GW of electricity, and the data centre's capacity is about 1 GW. The Western substation will allow electricity to be imported from the grid where required, for the data centre and battery storage.

Developer's positionCo-location reduces the electricity the data centre needs from the grid, and nearby offshore wind adds supply.

Points to raiseAsk for an hour-by-hour or annual energy balance showing how much of the data centre's use is met by on-site solar and how much comes from the grid.

Scoping Report §1.1.2, §4.4.23, §4.4.35; Leaflet p12, p14, p17, p20

Distance from homesIn the documents

The issueWhether the minimum distances between the scheme and homes are enough.

What the documents sayThe developer's minimum distances are 30 m from homes and other sensitive buildings to panels, 100 m to inverters and transformers, and 300 m to the data centre, substations and battery storage.

Developer's positionSite-specific screening and planting will be agreed with the nearest residents.

Points to raiseUse the Near me tab to find your own distances, and ask for larger buffers where you live.

Scoping Report §4.2.1

Cumulative effects and community benefitIn the documents

The issueSeveral other energy projects are coming forward around Walpole, and the community benefits are not yet defined.

What the documents sayThe developer notes "a number of projects" in development or construction near Walpole substation. A community benefit fund is proposed, but no amount has been given. The draft data centre policy says strategic data centres should agree a Local Benefit Plan, developed with the local community and linked to the development's size.

Developer's positionNear Walpole, the scheme's impact is limited to an underground cable close to the existing substation and an overhead line within the corridor of the existing lines.

Points to raiseAsk for a list of the projects included in the cumulative assessment, and for figures on community benefit.

Leaflet p19; Scoping Report §4.2.2; draft Data Centres NPS (Foreword, Local Benefit Plan)

Writing a useful letter

  • Letter writer: enter your postcode in the Near me tab and press Write a response letter for a draft built from your location and your own words.
  • Put the project name and reference at the top: Longmarsh Green Energy (EN0110048).
  • Give your name and postcode so your comments can be linked to a place.
  • Be specific about where. The Near me tab gives your distance to each part of the scheme.

Design commitments

  • Panels at least 30 m from homes and other sensitive buildings.
  • Inverters and transformers at least 100 m away.
  • Data centre, substations and battery storage at least 300 m away.
  • No overhead line tower within 100 m of a home.
  • 100 m buffer from Islington Heronry SSSI.
  • Construction HGVs routed away from Tilney St Lawrence, Barton Bendish and Fincham where possible.

About this map

This is an independent visualisation, not produced by the applicant. The published shapes were extracted from the vector layers of Scoping Report Appendix A, Figure 1-3 (Layout Plan, rev 01, 13/10/2026). They were placed on the map using the Ordnance Survey 1 km grid printed on the figures. Two figures placed separately agree to within 2.5 m, and the shapes follow field edges and the existing Walpole substation on aerial imagery. The applicant calls the boundary indicative.
